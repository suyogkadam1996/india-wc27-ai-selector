About This Project

What this is. A decision-support tool, not an official BCCI product. It doesn't have access to what real selectors use internally (ball-tracking speed/seam data, medical reports, dressing-room judgment) — it works entirely from free, public match data.

Data source. All stats come from Cricsheet, a free, ball-by-ball archive of international cricket. Multi-format profile stats (Test/T20I/IPL) come from the same source.

How the squad is picked. Every currently active player gets a suitability score built from recent form (weighted higher) and career stats. A constrained selection then fills 15 squad slots by role (wicketkeepers, batters, all-rounders, bowlers) using those scores.

How the Playing XI is picked. Chosen from the fixed 15 only, per venue — a player's score is adjusted using their own historical record at that specific ground where enough data exists (3+ innings), otherwise their general squad score is used unchanged.

Where human judgment overrides the numbers. A small, transparent override list lets real cricketing knowledge correct the data where it falls short — e.g. recognising a senior player's proven big-match temperament during a short-term form dip, or correcting a role the stats alone got wrong. Every override is explained in that player's reasoning, not hidden.

Known limitations. Batting average doesn't yet distinguish not-outs from dismissals (slightly understates everyone's average equally). Team assignment is inferred from batting appearances only. Some grounds have limited historical data, especially brand-new venues built for this tournament.
